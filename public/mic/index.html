The full cheat sheet

Read the safety rules first, then copy a prompt. Every prompt tells the AI to use only what you give it, and to say so when something is missing.

Before you paste anything

  1. Free and personal accounts can train on your chats. Switch it off: ChatGPT: Settings, Data controls, Improve the model for everyone. Claude: Settings, Privacy. Business plans don't train by default.
  2. Never paste names, phone numbers, medical details, live tender prices or a client's confidential documents into a personal account. Replace them with [placeholders]. WORK ACCOUNT moves need a work account (Copilot or Google Workspace) or a paid plan with training off.
  3. Connecting email, files or skills? Anything it reads can hide instructions for it. Use a dedicated folder, only skills you've read and trust, and never let it send, pay or delete without your yes.
  4. Recording a meeting or call? Tell everyone AI is listening first, and stop if anyone objects.
  5. AI drafts, you sign. Check every fact, figure and clause number. CDM duties, contract notices and golden thread records stay with the duty holder. People decisions stay with people.
  6. Running a firm? Write a one-page AI policy. RICS-regulated firms using AI must now have one.

WORK ACCOUNT: the move uses contract or tender documents, so use your work account (Copilot or Google Workspace) or a paid plan with training off. PAID PLAN: needs a paid Claude plan (Pro or above). Skills, Projects and connectors also work on the free plan.

Four upgrades to any prompt

  1. Say what done looks like. "Keep going until every line is checked or listed for me."
  2. Make it interview you. "Ask me one question at a time until you have what you need. Then draft."
  3. Make it mark its own homework. "Now list anything you guessed or weren't sure of."
  4. Find before you build. Ready-made skills and plugins: Claude, then Customize. Read one before you switch it on.

4Check your quantities

Tip. If the text won't highlight, it's a picture and AI is guessing. Use it to check your take-off, never to replace it.

Skills you can copy

A skill is a saved recipe for one job. Paste one of these into Claude and say: "Turn this into a skill, then ask me what else you need." Run it by typing / and its name. Skills are on every Claude plan (turn on code execution in Settings). In ChatGPT, paste it as a Project's instructions.

/our-rams

Drafts our RAMS in our template. Use for RAMS, method statements or risk assessments.

Draft RAMS in the format of the example I attach, using its headings and risk matrix. Follow the hierarchy of control and CDM 2015. Before drafting, ask me one question at a time about: task and sequence, access, height or depth, number of operatives, plant, what's nearby, and the permits we hold. Mark any site-dependent control [CHECK ON SITE]. Never invent permits, checks, test results, names or phone numbers. Finish with a list of everything you assumed. A competent person signs it off.

/site-report

Turns my voice notes and photos into our weekly site report.

Use my headings: summary, progress against programme, labour and plant, deliveries, health and safety, instructions and changes, delays and causes, next two weeks. Use only what I dictate or show in photos. Write NOT STATED under any heading I haven't covered. Keep delays factual, with dates, no blame, no promises. Plain UK English, short sentences. End with anything that could be a variation or delay notice, quoting my words, so I decide what to send.

/tender-answer

Writes tender answers in our winning structure from our past answers.

For each question: restate the criterion, then answer in our structure (what we do, how, who, evidence, result), within the word limit. Use only evidence in our project files. Write [EVIDENCE NEEDED] where we have none. Never invent projects, figures, dates or names. UK spelling, no filler. Then mark the answer against the criteria as a strict evaluator and say what would lift it. Give a word count for me to check in Word. A person leads the bid and signs it.

Level the quotes, with a citation on every line

QS, estimators, buyers WORK ACCOUNT

Use: Claude or ChatGPT with the PDFs attached, or Claude in Excel on a paid plan. Try it first on a package you've already awarded.

I attach the RFQ and three quotes for [package]. Use MY scope checklist below as the row headings. For every row and every bidder, give the price and quote the exact line or exclusion with its page number. Don't change any figure. Put anything missing, excluded or qualified on a separate 'gaps' list with a clarification question for each. Don't recommend an award. My checklist: [paste]

Tip. It can misread digits in a PDF, so check every price against the original. The same method works for revision A against revision B of a quote, spec or schedule: item, rev A wording, rev B wording, likely cost or programme effect.

Sort 100 tender queries in 30 minutes

QS, estimators WORK ACCOUNT

Use: Any AI chat with file upload, on an Excel export of the queries.

Here are [N] queries from [number] contractors as a table. Group duplicates and near-duplicates, give each group one clear question, say which role should answer it from this list [roles, not names], and flag any query that changes scope or price. Keep the original wording alongside.

Tip. Check every assignment before it goes out.

Audit the cost report before it goes out

QS, commercial managers WORK ACCOUNT PAID PLAN

Use: Claude in Excel, on workbooks you trust. People who build complex models say it follows the logic across sheets and finds mistakes they'd have missed.

Audit this workbook. Read only. List hard-coded numbers inside formula ranges, totals that don't include every row, lines where the total isn't quantity times rate, and formulas that change down a column. Change nothing; give the cell references.

Tip. It flags; you decide. Spreadsheets from outside the firm can hide instructions, so audit your own.

Variation or compensation event narrative

Contracts, QS, subcontractors

Use: Any AI chat. You supply the facts.

I'm a [main contractor / subcontractor] on a [project type] under [NEC4 ECC Option [ ] / JCT [ ]]. Write the narrative for a [variation / compensation event] notification dated [date]. Use only the facts I give you. What was instructed or happened: [ ]. Date, who instructed it, and how (email, meeting, drawing revision): [ ]. Documents it relates to: [ ]. Work affected: [ ]. Effect on cost: [amount, or 'not yet assessed']. Effect on programme: [days, or 'not yet assessed']. Set it out as: background, what changed, why it is a change to the contract, effect on cost, effect on programme, what I'm asking for. Don't invent facts, dates, amounts or instructions. Where something isn't given, write 'not stated'. Mark every clause number [CHECK CLAUSE] so I can check it against my contract. Factual tone, no blame, no threats.

Tip. You supply the facts; the AI only organises them. Some contracts, NEC4 included, set strict time limits for notifying a change, so check yours before you send. If you paste the instruction itself or contract wording, use a WORK ACCOUNT.

How the AI build works
  1. Free 30-minute call. We pick the three jobs that eat the most time: for example quotes, RAMS, tender answers, site diaries or client emails.
  2. The right platform, not a new subscription. Built in what you already pay for where that fits: Copilot agents in Microsoft 365, Projects and skills in Claude, Projects in ChatGPT Business, or Gemini in Google Workspace. I'll recommend whichever tool the evidence says suits you best.
  3. Each assistant is built on your own material. Your standards, tone and UK terms as its instructions; your templates, past winning answers, rates and policies as its knowledge (names and confidential details removed where needed); output in your own format. It's told never to invent figures, to flag what it's unsure of, and to leave sign-off to a named person.
  4. Tested on three real past jobs. Compared with what you actually sent, then tuned until it matches how you work.
  5. Handover. A 45-minute session for the people who will use it, a one-page guide per assistant, and a one-page AI use policy.
  6. Check-in after 30 days. Fix what is not working and tune for anything new.
