The full cheat sheet

Read the safety rules first, then copy a prompt. Every prompt tells the AI to use only what you give it, and to say so when something is missing.

Before you paste anything

  1. Free and personal paid accounts can learn from what you type. In ChatGPT, switch it off: Settings, Data controls, Improve the model for everyone. Business plans don't train on your data by default.
  2. On Microsoft 365 at work? Use Copilot Chat signed in with your work account.
  3. Never paste names, phone numbers, medical details, live tender prices or a client's confidential documents into a free tool. Use [placeholders] and fill them in after. Moves marked WORK ACCOUNT need your work account (Copilot or Google Workspace), or a paid plan with training switched off.
  4. Recording a meeting or call? Tell everyone before it starts, and stop if anyone objects.
  5. AI drafts, you sign. Check every fact, figure and clause number. CDM duties, contract notices and any higher-risk building records (golden thread) stay with the person who holds the duty.
  6. Running a firm? Put your rules in a one-page AI policy. RICS-regulated firms using AI must now have one.

WORK ACCOUNT means the move uses contract or tender documents. Use your work account (Copilot or Google Workspace), or a paid plan with training switched off.

Four upgrades most people miss

  1. Talk, don't type. Voice mode in ChatGPT or Gemini, or your phone's dictation, is faster than typing on site.
  2. Give it your files, not a summary. Projects, Gems and NotebookLM answer from your own documents.
  3. Make it interview you. "Ask me one question at a time until you have what you need. Then draft."
  4. Make it mark its own homework. "Now list anything you guessed or weren't sure of."

Eight AI moves to try on Monday

Start here

1Let AI take the minutes

Everyone

Use: Teams with Copilot or Teams Premium writes the recap for you. No licence? Use a notetaker such as Otter, Fireflies or Granola. Tell everyone first.

From this transcript, list the decisions, then a table of actions with owner and date. Write 'no owner agreed' where none was. Use only the transcript.

Tip. Names and numbers get misheard, so check the actions before you send them.

2Site diary and snags, by voice and photo

Site managers, supervisors

Use: Voice mode in ChatGPT or Gemini as you walk the site, with a photo of each defect. Snag apps such as Sitemate and Snag AI now do this inside your snag list.

I'm walking [project] on [date]. I'll talk and send photos. Write the site diary under: weather, labour by trade, work done, delays, deliveries, health and safety, instructions, tomorrow. For each photo, write a snag: location, trade, defect, suggested fix. Write 'NOT STATED' for anything I don't cover.

Tip. Keep the photos and the original notes: the diary may end up as evidence.

Contracts, RAMS and toolbox talks

3Ask your contract anything

QS, contracts, subcontractors WORK ACCOUNT

Use: NotebookLM or Copilot on your work account. Upload the subcontract, amendments and spec; every answer links to the clause it came from.

Make a table of every notice and payment deadline: what, who, how many days, what happens if it's missed, the clause number and its exact words. Write 'see particulars' or 'not stated' where the contract doesn't say. Then: the programme has slipped two weeks. What must I send, and by when?

Tip. Click each citation and read the clause before you rely on it.

4A RAMS assistant in your own format

H&S, site managers, subcontractors

Use: A ChatGPT Project or a Gemini Gem (both on free plans), or an agent in Copilot at work. Load your RAMS template, a good past RAMS and your site rules once. Paste this as its instructions:

Draft RAMS in our template only. Follow the hierarchy of control and CDM 2015. Before drafting, ask me about access, height or depth, operatives, plant and what's nearby. Mark site-dependent controls [CHECK ON SITE]. Never invent permits, checks or test results.

Tip. It's a first draft. A competent person makes it site-specific and signs it off.

5Toolbox talks in their language

Supervisors, H&S

Use: ChatGPT or Gemini, then voice mode to hear it read aloud in the second language.

Write a five-minute toolbox talk on [topic] from this near miss or RAMS: [paste, names removed]. Plain English, short sentences, three questions for the gang. Then give it again in [language].

Tip. Have a fluent speaker check the translation before you use it.

Winning work and getting paid

6Tender packs and take-offs

Estimators, QS, buyers WORK ACCOUNT

Use: Load the tender pack into NotebookLM or a Project. For measuring, AI take-off tools such as Kreo (UK) and Togal.AI measure areas and count items from PDF drawings.

From these tender documents, make a table of no more than 15 rows, highest price risk first: what's included, what's unclear (a tender query), and what's commonly missed. Give the document and page for each row. Label anything not in the documents 'general knowledge'.

Tip. Test a take-off tool on a job you've already priced before you trust it.

7A bid assistant that knows your best answers

Directors, bid writers WORK ACCOUNT

Use: A Project loaded with your past winning answers, case studies, policies and CVs. Run deep research in ChatGPT or Gemini on the client's priorities first.

Answer this question within [word limit] against these marking criteria: [paste]. Use only the evidence in this project. Write [EVIDENCE NEEDED] where we have none. Never invent projects, figures or dates. Count the words. Then mark it against the criteria as a strict evaluator would.

8Get paid on time

Sole traders, subcontractors

Use: Any chatbot. Put each final date for payment in your calendar when you apply, so the reminder goes out on time.

Write a friendly but firm payment reminder to [the main contractor's QS]. Application [number], £[amount], [project], submitted [date]. Payment due date [date], final date for payment [date]. I [have / have not] had a payment notice. Ask them to confirm the amount and payment date by [a working day]. Use only these facts. Under 120 words, no threats, no legal claims.

Tip. A reminder isn't a formal notice. Check your contract's payment steps. Homeowner jobs follow different rules.

Compare three subcontractor quotes

QS, estimators, buyers WORK ACCOUNT

Compare these three subcontractor quotes for [package] on a [project type], received by [date]. Use only the text I've pasted. Put them in a table: price, what is included, what is excluded, programme assumptions, qualifications. Where a quote doesn't say, write 'not stated'. Then list anything that makes the quotes not like-for-like. Don't recommend a winner. Quote A: [paste, names removed] Quote B: [paste, names removed] Quote C: [paste, names removed]

Tip. You decide the award. Check every price against the original quote.

Tender qualifications letter

QS, estimators WORK ACCOUNT

Draft a tender qualifications and clarifications letter for a [project type] tender due [date]. Use only the qualifications I list below, one numbered item each, in plain UK English. Don't add any qualification I haven't listed. If an item is unclear, write [CLARIFY WITH ME] beside it instead of guessing. My list: [paste, names removed]

Tip. Read it against the tender instructions before it goes. A qualification can make a bid non-compliant.

Contracts3 prompts, 3 tools

NEC4 and JCT administration: notices, variations, deadlines.

Also useful from the eight above: 3. Ask your contract anything.

Early warning notice (NEC4)

Contracts, QS, project managers

Act as a UK contract administrator. Using NEC4 ECC, draft an early warning notice from the Contractor to the Project Manager dated [date]. Use only these facts: what has happened: [ ]; date first known: [date]; likely effect on cost, Completion or Key Dates: [ ]. Where a fact is missing, write 'not stated'. Keep it factual and propose matters to discuss at a risk reduction meeting. Mark every clause number [CHECK CLAUSE] so I can check it against my contract. Don't invent facts, dates or amounts.

Tip. Check your contract's own wording and who must receive the notice before you send it.

Variation or compensation event narrative

Contracts, QS, subcontractors

I'm a [main contractor / subcontractor] on a [project type] under [NEC4 ECC Option [ ] / JCT [ ]]. Write the narrative for a [variation / compensation event] notification dated [date]. Use only the facts I give you. What was instructed or happened: [ ]. Date, who instructed it, and how (email, meeting, drawing revision): [ ]. Documents it relates to: [ ]. Work affected: [ ]. Effect on cost: [amount, or 'not yet assessed']. Effect on programme: [days, or 'not yet assessed']. Set it out as: background, what changed, why it is a change to the contract, effect on cost, effect on programme, what I'm asking for. Don't invent facts, dates, amounts or instructions. Where something isn't given, write 'not stated'. Mark every clause number [CHECK CLAUSE] so I can check it against my contract. Factual tone, no blame, no threats.

Tip. You supply the facts; the AI only organises them. Some contracts, NEC4 included, set strict time limits for notifying a change, so check yours before you send. If you paste the instruction itself or contract wording, use a WORK ACCOUNT.

Health and safety4 prompts, 3 tools

First drafts for a competent person to finish and sign.

Summarise a safety data sheet

H&S, supervisors

Below is the text of a safety data sheet for [product], used for [task] on a [type of site]. Summarise it for operatives in plain English: hazards, PPE, storage, first aid, spills, disposal. Use only the text I've pasted. Where the data sheet doesn't say, write 'not stated'. Then list questions I should ask the supplier. [paste data sheet text]

Tip. This does not replace your COSHH assessment. A competent person still signs that off.

Site induction script

H&S, site managers

Draft a ten-minute site induction script for a [type of site]. Use only the site rules I list: [list]. Where a standard topic (emergency arrangements, welfare, PPE, reporting) isn't covered by my list, write [ADD SITE DETAIL] instead of inventing a site-specific fact such as an assembly point, a first aider's name or a phone number. Plain English, short sentences, and five questions at the end to check understanding.

Tip. Fill every [ADD SITE DETAIL] on site before anyone is inducted.

Planners, site managers

Building safety2 prompts, 4 tools

Building Safety Act work: Gateway 2, the golden thread, records.

Gateway 2 contents check

Principal designers, project managers

I'm preparing a Gateway 2 submission for a higher-risk building. Here is the contents list of my documents, with the project name removed: [paste]. Compare it against the information the Building Safety Regulator expects for Gateway 2, and list anything missing or thin as a checklist. Use only my list for what I have. Don't quote regulations or document names from memory as fact. For each item, tell me which official guidance to check it against, and mark anything you're unsure of [CHECK GUIDANCE]. If my list is too vague to judge an item, write 'cannot tell from the list'.

Tip. This is a prompt for finding gaps, not for passing the gateway. The official guidance decides.

File tagging scheme for a register

Information managers, document controllers

Here is a list of our project files by name, with the project name removed: [paste]. Suggest a simple tagging scheme (document type, stage, date, owner) and a register layout as a table. Use only the file names I've pasted. Where you can't tell what a file is from its name, write 'unclear from name'. I'll decide what belongs in the golden thread.

Tip. The golden thread only covers higher-risk buildings. The duty stays with the person who holds it.

Specialist tools worth knowing

Subcontract enquiry

Buyers, QS

Write a subcontract enquiry for [package] on a [project type]. Use only the details below. Where one is missing, write [TO CONFIRM] instead of inventing it. Scope summary: [paste, names removed]. Programme window: [dates]. Attendances we provide: [ ]. Attendances they provide: [ ]. Insurances required: [ ]. Health and safety documents required: [ ]. Return date: [date]. Tip. Send it to your own team first. Anything they query, the subcontractors will too.

Supplier questionnaire

Buyers, directors

Draft a supplier questionnaire for [type of supplier] on a [project type]. Ask only about the topics I list: [list, for example insurance, health and safety record, financial standing, references, capacity]. Short plain questions, each with the evidence I should ask for. Don't add topics, and don't say any question is required by law, by the Procurement Act or by the Common Assessment Standard. I'll check that myself.

Tip. The Build UK Common Assessment Standard remains the pre-qualification backbone. Check against it.

Summarise supplier terms

Buyers, QS, directors WORK ACCOUNT

Here are a supplier's terms and conditions: [paste]. Summarise them in a table: payment terms, price changes, delivery, title (when ownership passes), liability limits, termination, anything unusual. Quote the words each row comes from. Use only the text I've pasted. Where the terms don't say, write 'not stated'.

Tip. Read the original clause for every row that matters. If it affects a large order, ask a solicitor.

  2. The right platform, not a new subscription. Built in what you already pay for where that fits: Copilot agents in Microsoft 365, custom GPTs or Projects in ChatGPT Business, Projects in Claude, or Gems in Google Workspace. I recommend whatever the evidence says works best for you.
  3. Each assistant is built on your own material. Instructions in your standards, tone and UK terms; your templates, past winning answers, rates and policies loaded as knowledge (names and confidential details removed where needed); output in your own format; rules to never invent figures, to flag what it is unsure of, and to leave sign-off to a named person.
  4. Tested on three real past jobs. Compared with what you actually sent, then tuned until it is right.
  5. Handover. A 45-minute session for the people who will use it, a one-page guide per assistant, and a one-page AI use policy.
  6. Check-in after 30 days. Fix what is not working and tune for anything new.
